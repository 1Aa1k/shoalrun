How

Click a rock to mark it. Click your own mark again to remove it.

Flip between years with space. A wave or a glint shows up in one year only. A rock sits in the same spot every year. That flip is the whole trick.

Infrared (i) is the giveaway for anything actually breaking the surface. Water swallows infrared, so open water goes black and dry rock glows. A shoal still under water stays dark -- so if it is bright in colour but dark in infrared, it is submerged, not exposed.

Shallow index (s) is the sandy-halo view. Blue light reaches deeper than green, so their ratio tracks how much bottom is showing through. Bright means shallow. A rock cluster usually sits inside a bright patch far bigger than the rocks themselves, so hunt the patch, then find the rocks in colour.

Only water more than 50 m off shore is shown, ordered so the busy water comes first. Stop whenever you like, it stays useful.
